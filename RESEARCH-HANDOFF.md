## 2. Regulatory facts (do not misstate)

From the FCA Handbook, [DISP 1.6](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/DISP/1/6.html) (as fetched):
- Prompt written acknowledgement with early reassurance the complaint is received and being dealt with (DISP 1.6.1R(1)(a)).
- The acknowledgement must say a written response will follow within 8 weeks of receipt (DISP 1.6.1R(1)(b)(ii)); keep the complainant informed of progress (1.6.1R(2)).
- By the end of 8 weeks: a final response (accept / offer redress / reject with reasons, enclosing the FOS leaflet and referral) or a written holding response explaining why and when a final response is expected, plus FOS referral right (DISP 1.6.2R).
- EMD/PSD complaints are shorter: 15 business days, or 35 in exceptional cases (DISP 1.6.2AR).
- **The "4-week" point did NOT appear on this page.** Do not claim it. Verify any 4-week/summary-resolution wording against DISP 1.5 before use.
- Records: keep a record of each complaint and measures taken, 3 years generally, 5 for UCITS collective portfolio management (DISP 1.9.1R, per search summary of [handbook](https://www.Handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/DISP/1/9.pdf); **unverified on page**). Motor finance has special preservation rules, check separately.
- FOS page I fetched ([financial-ombudsman.org.uk/businesses/complaints-deal](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/businesses/complaints-deal)) states none of the deadline or record-keeping detail. "What FOS expects firms to record" is **not verified**; rely on DISP 1.9 / DISP Sch 1 only.
- Non-financial small firms: no single statutory complaints deadline found; I found no source stating otherwise, but sector ombudsmen vary. The "no single deadline" claim is **unverified** by a page; phrase it as "depends on your sector and any ombudsman scheme".
- Complaint-ombudsman referral time limits quoted by a firm page (6 months) are firm-specific, **unverified** for rule purposes.

**Must NOT be promised on the site:** "FCA compliant", "DISP compliant", "guarantees you meet the 8-week deadline", "stops ombudsman referrals", "regulatory advice". Safe wording: "tools that help you track the dates your own procedure sets", "built by someone with 5 years of UK complaints experience", "you remain responsible for compliance; this is not legal or regulatory advice". Founder's experience is a credibility claim; keep it factual and verifiable.

## 5. Pricing verdict

**Delivery hours** (intake form, acknowledgement email, case log with computed deadline dates, escalation alert, monthly summary; on client's existing tools): discovery/mapping 2-3h, build 6-10h, testing with sample cases 2h, handover doc + 30-min walkthrough 1.5-2h. **Range 12-17h**, realistic 14h incl. rework; first build is longer, repeat builds from a template ~8-10h.

**Effective rate at £795:** £47-66/h (about £57 at 14h). For a tool-setup freelancer that is fine; for someone with regulated-complaints expertise it underprices the knowledge (the real asset), and leaves no margin for client delays or scope creep.

**Client running costs/month (published list prices, USD as shown):**
- Zapier Pro 750 tasks: $19.99/mo annual, $29.99 monthly; Free is 100 tasks ([zapier.com/pricing](https://www.zapier.com/pricing)). Enough for a small firm's complaint volume.
- Airtable Team $20/user/mo annual; Business $45; Free exists ([airtable.com/pricing](https://www.airtable.com/pricing)).
- HubSpot Service Hub: Free for 2 users; Starter $7-20/seat ([hubspot.com](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/service)).
- Make: price not retrievable, **unverified** (Free has 1,000 credits).
- Google Sheets/Forms: effectively free on existing Workspace (**unverified**, not checked).
- Typical total: £0-£40/month. Compare Complyr at £89/user/mo (about £1,068/user/yr).

**Commercial critique:**
- £795 is a good "low-risk first yes" for a niche where nobody publishes prices, and it undercuts the Complyr 12-month cost for even one user. It is cheap for a firm that could face an ombudsman case.
- Weak points: demand unproven; fixed price with unlimited tool variety (Sheets vs Airtable vs HubSpot) invites scope creep; no recurring revenue; the buyer will ask "does it make us compliant?" which you cannot promise.
- Solo-studio risk: one-off £795 jobs need ~1 sale per week to be a business; recurring care matters more than the setup price.

**Recommendation: keep £795 only as a tightly scoped entry; add tiers rather than raising the base.**
- **Setup £795:** one tool stack (client's choice, one of Sheets/Airtable/HubSpot), 1 intake form, acknowledgement email, case log with date fields, 1 escalation alert, monthly summary, 1 revision round, 30-min handover. Fixed scope list, stated hours cap (~14h).
- **Plus £1,495:** adds your complaint process pack (stage definitions, templates for acknowledgement/holding/final response drafts reviewed by you as general templates, not legal advice), root-cause tagging, two alert rules, 60-min training. Expected 24-28h, about £55-60/h, but higher value from expertise.
- **Care £49-£99/month (suggest £79):** tool monitoring, small fixes, monthly summary check, 1h change allowance. Price is a guess to test; it is not benchmarked.
- If you get 3 sales at £795 with no pushback, raise Setup to £995 (price discovery, not research-backed).

Next step: 10 buyer conversations before more building (mom-test skill), then run mw-council on the offer.

## 7. Comparable services for the four complaints packages (research of 19 Oct 2026)

**What regulators say good looks like (what buyers need)**
- FCA DISP 1.3.3: firms must analyse causes of complaints to find root causes common to types of complaint, consider whether they affect other products/processes, and keep records of senior-personnel analysis and decisions on MI ([FCA Handbook DISP 1](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/disp1)).
- FCA thematic review: good firms had dashboards, data packs setting out root causes and actions, and triangulated with external sources such as social media ([FCA good/poor practice](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/complaints-and-root-cause-analysis-good-practice-and-areas-improvement)).
- DISP 1.10/1.10A (reporting) and Financial Ombudsman / Ombudsman Association "learning from complaints" pages were not fetched: **unverified**. Check before quoting.

**(b) Pay for vs commodity (judgement, from the above; not buyer interviews)**
- Buyers pay for: root cause with action and owner (DISP wording), a board pack a non-specialist can act on, and an honest recommendation list. Records of senior decisions are an FCA expectation, so the deck and action tracker carry real value.
- Commodity: Excel trackers, flowcharts, checklists, infographics. A freelance dashboard is ~$45-$180 on Fiverr. Sell the thinking, not the files.

**(e) Data protection must-dos (small processor, UK)**
- Get a written contract (controller instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processors, deletion/return, audit help) before receiving any records: [ICO contracts and liabilities](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/what-needs-to-be-included-in-the-contract/). Processors also have direct duties: [ICO processor liabilities](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/responsibilities-and-liabilities-for-processors-in-their-own-right/); overview: [controllers and processors](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/controllers-and-processors/).
- Ask clients to anonymise before sending (the "anonymised cases" in the Health Check). Key-coded or pseudonymised data is still personal data ([ICO anonymisation intro](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/introduction-to-anonymisation/)).
- If pseudonymising, keep the key separate from the data ([ICO pseudonymisation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/pseudonymisation/)).
- Set and state a retention period in the contract; delete or anonymise at project end, including backups ([ICO storage limitation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/storage-limitation/)).
- Practical: encrypted storage, no personal data in AI tools or chat without contract cover, no sub-processors without client approval. Security/ICO registration (fee) status for Maz Works: **unverified**.
